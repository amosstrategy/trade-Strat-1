# America on one page: take-home, rent, inflation — and what “broke” actually means

**Scope:** United States only · July 2026  
**Method:** same corrections as the Germany one-pager — **gross ≠ net**, **realistic rent**, **inflation when comparing years**, **buffer = insolvency risk**

---

## 0. Definition first (the thing the soft line skipped)

**Legal bankruptcy (US):** Chapter 7 / Chapter 13 — a court process when you cannot pay debts as they come due (or need a court plan).  
**Economic meaning (plain English):** you cannot pay what you must pay when it is due — rent, food, card minimum, medical bill — without new credit, charity, or skipping something essential.

**Fed SHED 2025:** only **63%** would cover a **$400** shock with cash/equivalent. **37%** would not. **12%** could not pay $400 by any means. **16%** did not pay all bills last month.

That is not “the country filed Chapter 7.” It is: **a huge share of households are already one missed paycheck or one bill away from Zahlungsunfähigkeit** — the German legal test (§ 17 InsO: unable to meet *due* payment obligations). Same logic, different court.

| Layer | Share (US, approx.) | Meaning |
|-------|---------------------|---------|
| No $400 cash buffer | **~37%** of adults | Must borrow/sell/skip |
| Cannot pay $400 at all | **~12%** | Hard insolvency of a tiny shock |
| Missed bills last month | **~16%** | Already failing due payments |
| Top liquidity / top income | **~top 10–20%** | Buffer exists; narrative “all fine” lives here |

**So:** saying “America is not broke” about *GDP/Treasury* while **~⅓–⅔** of adults fail a $400 test is the same sleight of hand as “Deutschland ist nicht pleite” next to the **1.300 €** Destatis buffer. Sovereign ≠ household.

## 1. Gross ≠ take-home

Median household income **$83,730** (Census 2024) is **before** many people think in “paycheck.” For a worker:

| Gross / month (single, rough) | Fed income + FICA (~7.65%) | + typical state/health | **Take-home (illustrative)** |
|------------------------------|----------------------------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| $3,000 | −FICA −fed | −state/benefits | **~$2,300–2,500** |
| $4,000 (~$48k/yr) | −~$300 FICA −~$300 fed | −state 0–8% −premiums | **~$2,900–3,400** (no state / no benefits) → often **~$2,400–2,900** with CA/NY + health |
| $5,000 | … | … | **~$3,400–3,900** before benefits; often less |
| $6,970 (~median HH /12) | household, often 2 earners | … | not one person’s net |

**US quirk vs Germany:** payroll tax is lower (~7.65% employee FICA vs ~21% German SV), but **health insurance premiums** and **deductibles** hit the paycheck or the residual hard. “Take-home” before rent is not “money to live.”

## 2. Realistic rent (not the comforting average)

National asking rents often **~$1,700–1,800**/month. HUD Fair Market Rent (modest, 40th percentile) still **~$960 1BR / ~$1,175 2BR** national average — and **metros much higher** (top-50 metro 1BR FMR up **~40%** FY2021→FY2026).

| Take-home (approx.) | Realistic rent | Burden | **Left** |
|---------------------|----------------|--------|----------|
| **~$2,000** | **~$1,000–1,200** | **50–60%** | **~$800–1,000** |
| ~$2,500 | ~$1,200–1,500 | ~50–60% | ~$1,000–1,300 |
| ~$3,200 (tighter city) | ~$1,600–2,000 | ~50–60% | ~$1,200–1,600 |
| Median HH $83.7k (~$7k gross/mo, 2 adults) | ~$1,800–2,200 | ~25–35% of HH | more room — **for two** |

**Same German pattern:** “average rent burden ~30%” hides the city single on **half of take-home**.

## 3. Inflation — do not compare 2020 dollars to 2026 life

BLS CPI-U (chain of Dec–Dec rates ≈ **+24–25%** all-items Dec 2020 → Dec 2025). Sticky pieces:

| Category | Rough cumulative since late 2020 | Why it matters |
|----------|----------------------------------|----------------|
| **All items** | **~+24–25%** | Nominal wages look better than they feel |
| **Food** | **~+22%+** (and more in 2021–23 spike years) | Hits the residual every week |
| **Rent / shelter** | **Rent of primary residence ~+30%** class of gains; metro FMRs often **+35–40%** | Largest fixed bill |
| **Energy** | Volatile (gasoline spiked then fell); utilities still elevated in many years | Heat/AC/gas = Germany’s lesson |
| **Medical care (CPI)** | **~+12%** index — **understates** household burden | Premiums, deductibles, skipped care |
| **Health system $** | NHE **$5.3T**, **$15,474/person**, **18% GDP** (CMS 2024) | Same as DE Pflege/GKV: system cost ≠ CPI basket |

**Rule:** any 2021 income or 2020 rent “average” without CPI adjustment is **too optimistic** for 2026 affordability.

## 4. Who can pay $110 / $220 / $550 / $5,500 per month? (€100–€5,000)

After **tax + rent + inflated food/energy/health**, not after “median income”:

| Price | Households that could *without clear distress* (estimate) |
|-------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **~$110 (€100)** | **~20–30%** — not the no-$400-buffer bloc |
| **~$220 (€200)** | **~10–18%** |
| **~$550 (€500)** | **~$4–8%** |
| **~$5,500 (€5,000)** | **~1–2%** / employers |

If take-home **$2,000** and rent **$1,000**, residual **$1,000** — a **$110** subscription is **11%** of life money. Same math as Germany.

## 5. Insolvency stack (US numbers)

| Signal | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| No $400 cash/equivalent | **37%** of adults | Fed SHED 2025 |
| Cannot pay $400 any way | **12%** | Fed SHED 2025 |
| Missed bills prior month | **16%** | Fed SHED 2025 |
| Behind on rent (renters) | **23%** sometime in past year | Fed SHED 2025 |
| Household debt | **$18.8T**; cards **$1.25T** | NY Fed Q1 2026 |
| Aggregate delinquency | **4.8%** of balances (mortgage-heavy) | NY Fed |
| Card early-delinquency transition | **~8.6%** annualized | NY Fed |
| Student loans 90+ days | **~10.3%** of balances | NY Fed |

**Credit is the American buffer.** Germany uses transfers + lower household debt/GDP; the US uses **cards**. Both can look “solvent” until the buffer (savings *or* new credit) fails.

---

## 6. One sentence

**America’s GDP is not in Chapter 7. A large minority of Americans already fail due bills or a $400 shock — which is the economic definition of insolvency, whether or not a lawyer has filed.** After take-home tax, **$1,000–1,800 rents**, and **~25%+ cumulative CPI** since 2020, premium monthly services are for the buffered minority — not for the people the averages hide.
