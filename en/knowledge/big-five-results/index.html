Understanding your Big Five results

You took the IPIP-NEO-120 and now face numbers: "mean 3.4/5", "percentile ~72", "high", 30 facets. Here is what every field means in plain language — and why no score is "better".

Daniel Klotz · Paradise Ventures · July 2026

The three numbers on every field

Mean (x/5): the average of your 1–5 answers for that area. 3 = neutral. Reverse-keyed items are flipped automatically before scoring.

Percentile (~x): how you place against Johnson's internet norm sample (>600,000 people). Percentile 72 = about 72% score lower, 28% higher. A placement, not a grade — and our values are approximations from published distribution parameters.

High / average / low: rough banding of the mean (above ~3.35 high, below ~2.65 low). No level is better — every trait level has strengths and costs.

The five domains

  • Neuroticism — emotional reactivity. High = sensitive alarm system; low = stable baseline. Not a clinical judgment.
  • Extraversion — outward energy. High = sociable, assertive; low = reserved, recharges alone.
  • Openness — cognitive and aesthetic breadth. High = curious, questioning; low = practical, grounded.
  • Agreeableness — cooperation vs. competition. High = trusting, helpful; low = skeptical, confrontation-ready.
  • Conscientiousness — structure and self-control. High = organized, reliable; low = flexible, spontaneous.

The 30 facets — the real resolution

Each domain splits into 6 facets (4 items each). Two people with the same domain score can differ completely at facet level — that is why we use the full 120-item instrument and require 120/120 answers before scoring. In the app, every facet card expands with a plain-language explanation.

What the results are not

  • Not a type — there are no Big Five "types".
  • Not a diagnosis — self-report for reflection only.
  • Not destiny — traits are stable but movable over years.
  • Not exact — 5–10 percentile points difference is noise.

The HD bridge

The app shows exploratory notes placing your Big Five profile next to your Human Design chart. Explicitly exploratory — no validated mapping exists. That gap is what our research export investigates: research design.
